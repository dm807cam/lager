# Limits

The limits Lager enforces and what it deliberately does not do.

## Enforced limits

- **Storage per instance:** The data of an instance is limited to `LAGER_INSTANCE_QUOTA_MB` (default 50 MiB), unless an administrator set other storage for its app or for a group of its owner (see [Storage per group and per app](#storage-per-group-and-per-app)). This size includes the documents, the uploaded files (`assets`) and the pages an instance saved itself (`page`). Documents that came from outside have a quota of their own (see "Data from outside" below). When the limit is reached, Lager refuses further writes, uploads and saves with the error `quota_exceeded` (HTTP 507 for the database); reading and deleting keep working. Versions created by deploys, snapshots and the trash are not counted; see [Storage outside the quota](#storage-outside-the-quota).
- **Instances per user:** Every account that is not an administrator may own at most as many instances as an administrator sets in the administration area under Settings (1 to 1000, default 20). This applies when creating further instances, including self-service.
- **New instances per day:** Accounts that are not administrators may create at most 10 instances within 24 hours; deleted instances count, and so does the instance created by the first deploy of an app. This stops anyone from filling the storage by creating, filling and deleting in a loop.
- **Instances for other accounts:** Whoever deploys an app may create instances of it that belong to someone else. Each one counts against the creator's 10 per day, never against the receiver's. An account that is not an administrator may create at most 5 instances for other accounts within 24 hours, and none for an account that already owns half or more of the instances it may own (10 with the default of 20): the rest of that limit is for the account itself. Lager refuses with `conflict` (HTTP 409) and says to ask that person or an administrator. Administrators are not limited in what they hand out, up to the receiver's limit.
- **Open connections:** Per viewer and instance at most 8 live-update streams for the database and 8 for rooms; for anonymous visitors the viewer is the client address, and all of them together may hold at most 100 per instance. Further ones get HTTP 429, and the page works without live updates there.
- **Database requests:** Per signed-in viewer and instance at most 600 reads (`get`, query) and 300 writes (`set`, `update`, `add`, `delete`, `acquire`) per minute. Anonymous visitors are counted per client address, where a whole IPv6 /64 network is one address: 600 reads and 150 writes per minute, so one address never gets more than one account. All anonymous visitors of an instance together get eight times that (4800 reads and 1200 writes per minute), so many addresses together stay bounded; someone who uses this up slows live updates for the other guests of that instance, not for signed-in people, and the others' own budgets are not used up meanwhile. Further requests get HTTP 429 with the error `rate_limited` and `Retry-After: 5`. The browser runtime reports it to the app as `unavailable`; live queries (`onSnapshot`) wait as long as the answer says and then retry by themselves. A live query reloads at most every 250 ms: changes that arrive faster are answered by one reload. Lager tells visitors apart by the client address the proxy reports, so `LAGER_TRUST_PROXY` must match your setup (see [Installation](installation.md)); otherwise all anonymous visitors share one address.
- **Data from outside:** An app may declare 8 ingest endpoints, and a request to one is at most 64 KiB (4 KiB unless the endpoint says otherwise). Per minute an instance takes 150 requests from one client address without a token (an IPv6 /64 is one address), 600 per endpoint and 1200 in all; an endpoint also takes at most 10000 documents per day (UTC) unless it declares another number up to 100000. A visitor who adds documents under an `anyone` rule sends at most 4096 bytes at a time, and all visitors together add at most 600 documents per minute and 10000 per day under one rule. Further requests get HTTP 429 with `Retry-After`. What came in is kept in a file of its own per instance and may take `LAGER_INGEST_QUOTA_MB` there (default 10 MiB, `0` = no instance takes data from outside), apart from the instance's storage and measured in the pages the file really holds; when it is full, an endpoint answers 507, or drops its oldest received documents if it declares `"onFull": "rotate"`. These counters, like all request limits, are kept in memory and start again with the server. See [Receiving data from outside](building-apps.md#receiving-data-from-outside).
- **Queries:** At most 10 `where` clauses, 5 `orderBy` fields, 30 values in the list of an `in`, `not-in` or `array-contains-any` clause, and 1000 results (`limit`). A query beyond that is refused with `invalid_argument`.
- **Paths and documents:** Paths are limited to 16 segments and 1000 bytes, and a segment to 200 bytes of letters, digits and `_ - . ~ : @ +` (not `.` or `..`); the server and the browser runtime enforce the same rule, so every stored document stays reachable from the app. A document is at most 256 KiB.
- **Domains:** At most 10 custom domains per instance.
- **Saved versions:** An instance keeps its last 20 self-saved pages; older ones are deleted.
- **Restores and version changes:** Restoring a snapshot, activating another version by hand and switching automatic updates on (when that changes the version) each take a snapshot first. Together they are limited to 10 per instance and hour, in the dashboard, the REST API and the MCP tools alike; further ones get the error `rate_limited` (HTTP 429). Only what really takes a snapshot counts: a refused or failed attempt, a change on an instance without data and deploys do not.
- **Open databases:** Lager keeps the databases of at most 64 instances open at a time (and as many asset indexes) and closes the least recently used one; it is reopened on the next request. This is not configurable and needs no attention.
- **Snapshots:** They contain only the instance's own documents. `restore_snapshot` does not restore uploaded files, self-saved pages or documents received from outside. Only the owner of the instance can restore one, in the dashboard or with `restore_snapshot`: a restore rolls back all documents, including other people's private areas and data the app's rules keep from managers.
- **Reserved names:** The name of a deleted instance stays reserved for its former owner. Other accounts cannot take it over, because otherwise stored browser data and service workers of the old instance would carry over to the new one. New apps and instances also cannot take a name from a fixed list of names that stand for parts of a site (such as `www`, `api`, `admin`, `login`, `accounts`, `mail`, `status` or `support`), nor the platform's own name when the platform address lies under the apps domain. Apps and instances that already have such a name keep working.

- **Deploys:** An app takes at most 60 new versions per hour, and one person starts at most 60 new apps per hour, in the REST API and the MCP tools together (accounts that are not administrators are held to the 10 new instances per day above first). A deploy that is refused or changes nothing does not count. Beyond the limit a deploy answers `409` with the reason `deploy_rate`.

- **Title, description and note:** The title of an app has at most 100 characters, its description 200 and the note of a version 500. Each is one line of plain text: line breaks and runs of spaces become a single space, and control characters are refused.

- **Language model calls:** A call to `lager.complete` is given up after 90 seconds by default (`LAGER_AI_TIMEOUT_SECONDS`, 5 to 600). Keep the value below the response timeout of the reverse proxy.

## Storage per group and per app

In the administration area, an administrator can set the storage per instance for:

- **An app** (Apps, the field next to each app): applies to every instance of that app, whoever owns it.
- **A group** (Groups, the group's page): applies to every instance owned by a member of the group.

The value is in MiB, from 1 to 1048576 (1 TiB); an empty field follows the next rule again. Lager uses the first of these that is set: the app's storage, then the largest storage of the groups the instance's owner belongs to (being in one more group never takes space away), then `LAGER_INSTANCE_QUOTA_MB`. A setting can be smaller than the default. Lowering it below what an instance already uses deletes no data: the instance keeps its documents, files and saved pages, and further writes, uploads and saves are refused until enough has been deleted. Snapshots are the exception: all snapshots of an instance may take 3 times its storage, but never less than 3 times `LAGER_INSTANCE_QUOTA_MB`. A setting below the default therefore removes no snapshots, but after lowering a larger setting (or when the owner leaves a group with one) the older snapshots beyond the new budget are removed at the instance's next snapshot; the two protected snapshots stay (see [Storage outside the quota](#storage-outside-the-quota)). The instance page shows the storage that applies to the instance, and the apps overview shows it for every instance, with whether it was set for the app or by which group. When an instance changes hands, the new owner's groups count.

## Storage outside the quota

Four things take disk space without counting against `LAGER_INSTANCE_QUOTA_MB`. Plan the disk for them:

- **Data from outside:** Documents an instance received through ingest endpoints or from visitors under an `anyone` rule lie in `inbox.sqlite` next to its data and take up to `LAGER_INGEST_QUOTA_MB` (default 10 MiB) per instance on top of its quota, plus 24 KiB for the empty file. They are not part of snapshots.

- **Snapshots:** Lager takes one before every version change of an instance (a deploy to an instance that follows deploys, a manual activation) and before every restore. An instance keeps its newest `LAGER_SNAPSHOT_KEEP` snapshots (default 20), and all of them together may take at most 3 times the instance quota (150 MiB with the default quota; for storage set per app or group, 3 times that storage, but never less than 3 times the default); older ones are deleted first. Two snapshots are never deleted for this and are counted first: the newest one, and the newest one that is at least 24 hours old. Several version changes in a row on a nearly full instance therefore still leave a state from before that day, and with `LAGER_SNAPSHOT_KEEP=1` two snapshots can remain. The total stays at 3 times the quota, because the two protected snapshots are part of that budget (it is exceeded only if those two alone are larger, which the quota rules out unless it was lowered afterwards). A snapshot holds the documents only, so it is never larger than the quota. The instance page shows the space its snapshots take.
- **Trash:** A deleted instance moves to the trash with its documents, files, snapshots and saved pages, and stays there for `LAGER_TRASH_DAYS` days (default 30). The limit on new instances per day bounds how fast the trash can grow.
- **Deployed versions:** Every deploy with new content stores one HTML file of at most 16 MiB. An app keeps its newest 100 deployed versions; an older one is deleted for good with the next deploy, unless an instance still runs it. The worst case for one app is therefore 100 versions plus one per instance that is pinned to an older one.

## What Lager cannot do

- No sign-in through external providers (single sign-on); accounts use email and password.
- The quota applies to the data of an instance (documents, uploaded files, self-saved pages), not to deployed versions, snapshots or the trash (see [Storage outside the quota](#storage-outside-the-quota)). There is no limit on the total size.
- Snapshots back up only the instance's own documents, not uploaded files, not self-saved pages and not documents received from outside.
- The trash is emptied after `LAGER_TRASH_DAYS` days (default 30) and cannot be restored through Lager.
- Only the building blocks `db`, `user`, `permissions`, `assets`, `room`, `page`, `downloads` and `ingest`; all other names return `null`, and `lager.complete` needs a model the administrator connects.
- Ingest endpoints take JSON objects by `POST` only: no form posts, no file uploads, no `GET` pixels, and no check of a sender's own signature (a webhook is told apart by the endpoint's token alone). The websites that may call an endpoint are part of the app's declaration, not a setting of the instance.
- Realtime rooms live only in memory: no history, nothing survives a restart, and several Lager processes do not share rooms.
- For custom domains, Lager does not check who owns the host name.
- No TLS of its own: HTTPS is handled by the proxy in front.
- No automatic backup and no replication.
